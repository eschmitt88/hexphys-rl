six playable greyboxes · rubric prototype gate · juice off by design

Six games. No art. Is the loop fun?

Each station below is a complete core loop on the shared engine — a goal, the decision that matters, a score, an end state — rendered as plainly as possible on purpose. The fun rubric's prototype gate asks one question first: strip everything and toggle the juice off — is the thirty-second loop worth repeating? Play each for five minutes and answer that, per game, before anything else.

01 · foundry contracts · puzzle

Design a reactor to spec

Click chamber cells to place walls; click a port to shut it (free). A enters at the blue port, B at the orange one, product must leave through the gold ones. Commission runs the fine simulation; the score is the fraction of exiting mass that is product. Pass the target within the wall budget to unlock the next contract.

02 · watershed · continent strategy

Feed five towns through eight seasons

Two rivers (blue) pulse: wet phase, dry phase. Five towns (triangles) each need a season's worth of water — the bar fills as they drink. Any tile above the flood line turns red and costs you. You get six builds per season: reservoirs hold three times the water and smooth the pulse, dams steer, pipes pass flow along one axis only. Score: 10 per fed town, minus flood damage.

03 · clockwork · oscillator performance

Bring ninety-one clocks into one beat

Every tile is a chemical oscillator; colour is phase, grey tiles are dead (their material is below the threshold). Impure material drifts. Purify a tile to fix its frequency (10 uses), plant a pacemaker that couples four times harder (2 uses), or tap a tile to snap it to its neighbours' phase — a manual phase-lock. Raise the coupling slider and the lattice locks easier but slower. Win by holding synchrony R ≥ 0.85 for 200 beats.

04 · the descent · exploration roguelike

Escape a flooding machine

You are the red dot inside a randomly generated ported machine that has been running for a while. Water flows from hidden sources to hidden drains; dark blue cells are flooded and drown you slowly. Reach the green tile. Click an adjacent cell to step (or focus the board and use d e w a z x for the six directions). Amber cells are valves: click one to open it and let a flooded passage drain — or hold Shift and click an open valve to shut it and stop a flood upstream. Gold diamonds are castings worth salvaging.

05 · creep wars · real-time strategy

Drown the hive before it takes the board

Purple creep grows and spreads through open ports from its hives (dark purple); every so often it seeds a new hive on the frontier where the ground is driest. Water kills creep, and a hive under three units of head drowns. You earn a credit every 150 ticks: pumps (5) push water, dams (2) block both water and creep, reservoirs (3) store. The slider is the creep's growth rate — the AI's difficulty. Win when every hive has drowned; lose at 65% coverage.

06 · the inspector · deduction

Four faults, eight inspections

One hundred and twenty-seven components run on their certified behavioural models. Four of them are lying — clogged or leaking — and the clogs get worse while you wait. You can't see the truth; you see the residual: gauge readings minus what the models predict, red where the real world holds less water than it should, blue where it holds more. Read the pattern (a clog starves everything downstream; a leak makes a local sink), then click a tile to inspect it. A fault found is repaired on the spot. Eight inspections, no more.

how to playtest these

Score the loop, not the sim

Rubric protocol, prototype gate: for each game answer G1 (is the 30-second loop worth repeating with no art?) and G2 (is there a real decision between a blind guess and a solved line?), then rate learning & mastery, challenge–skill balance, and feel 0–4. Two or three raters, independently, before discussing. Everything here is deliberately unjuiced — that is the test, not a limitation.
