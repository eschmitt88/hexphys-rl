# Six games for the hexphys engine

Six deliberately different games, all buildable on what the engine now
does: ported hex tiles with a component library (SPICE loop), flow +
chemistry + oscillators, a measured four-level zoom (17,353 cells -> 469
-> 21 -> 3 nodes at ~1% error), and detail-where-you-look adaptive
fallback. Scored against the game-fun-rubric v0.5 **prototype gate only**
(G1, G2, dims 1/3/4) from **design intent** (S2 first perspective) — these
are hypotheses to greybox, not results.

Constraint from the user, read through the rubric: **large maps, especially
at high levels.** Big maps score on the psychosocial tier (5.1 goal
hierarchy across timescales, 6.3 information gaps, 2.5 self-directed play,
7.1 fantasy scale, 6.2 emergence) but are taxed on the functional tier that
gates it (3.4 dead time, 4.4 state legibility, 8.2 interface cost, 8.1
onboarding). Functional floor < 2.0 makes psychosocial scores unreliable.
So a large map is fun only if legibility and dead time are solved — and the
engine's zoom is precisely that mechanism (altitude = attention; the coarse
level is the glanceable state). Design rule: **the zoom belongs inside the
core loop, not in an options menu.**

## 1. Watershed — continent-scale hydraulic strategy

- **Genre**: strategy-sim (Timberborn / Frostpunk / SimCity lineage).
- **Fantasy (7.1/7.3)**: the hydraulic engineer of a living continent. Skill
  depends on the fantasy and vice versa: you win by reading water.
- **Map**: campaign from an L2 board (61 tiles) to L4 (7^3 regions; ~10^5
  cells). The top level is always the play surface; you descend to fix.
- **30-second loop (G1)**: a season pulse arrives -> the flow map shows a
  region starving or flooding -> zoom -> re-plumb one component -> zoom
  out -> watch the correction propagate.
- **Interesting decision (G2)**: buffer vs throughput (reservoirs vs pipes);
  certified vs improvised components (cheap + predictable vs flexible +
  audited); where this season's build budget goes.
- **Distinctive agency (2.6)**: hold a whole continent's flow in one glance
  and reach down to one valve.
- **Physics used**: flow, drying, pulsed sources, Tier-2 clogging as decay.
- **Biggest rubric risk**: 3.4 dead time between seasons; 4.4 legibility at
  scale (the zoom must carry it); 2.6 could read as generic city-builder
  verbs.
- **Profile (S1)**: Mastery-Strategy + Creativity-Design; achievement play.
- **Prototype gate (intent)**: G1 3 · G2 4 · D1 3 · D3 2 · D4 3.

## 2. Foundry Contracts — the certified-engineer puzzle

- **Genre**: open-ended puzzle (Opus Magnum / SpaceChem lineage).
- **Fantasy**: a master engineer whose castings are trusted at every scale.
- **Map**: single tile at first; every tier's contract board is built from
  YOUR earlier castings, so late contracts run on L3 boards (147 tiles) —
  the map grows because the parts grow.
- **Loop**: design -> commission (the shakedown run is the dramatic beat)
  -> score card (yield %, footprint, cost) -> refine. Solutions shareable;
  leaderboard histograms.
- **Decision**: the Damkohler tension made a design choice — mixer vs
  pipe; footprint vs yield; sealed component vs risky contraption.
- **Agency**: the spec sheet IS the object — you can *describe* what you
  built in six numbers and a scale.
- **Physics**: chemistry A+B->C, valves, pumps, later oscillators.
- **Risk**: 5.4 pacing flat (puzzle-game monotone); 6.1 rate of new
  mechanics; 2.4 must avoid a single solved line (histograms help).
- **Profile**: Mastery-Strategy + Achievement-Completion; striving play
  (contracts are disposable ends).
- **Gate (intent)**: G1 4 · G2 4 · D1 4 · D3 3 · D4 3. Loop already
  exists on docs/ports.html.

## 3. Clockwork — conducting a lattice of living clocks

- **Genre**: rhythm/performance + construction.
- **Fantasy**: conductor of a machine that keeps time.
- **Map**: large by design — phase-lock waves crossing thousands of clocks
  is the spectacle, and the measured engineered-coherence result makes it
  exact and cheap at scale (uniform-material chambers = SPICE-exact
  oscillators, 0.97x amp / 1.00x freq / r=0.94).
- **Loop**: place/tune an oscillator component, couple it, watch sync
  spread; nudge phase in real time (a manual PLL — tap to resync).
- **Decision**: coupling strength vs frequency spread (the Kuramoto
  criterion as a design choice); where the pacemaker goes; purity budget
  (uniform material is scarce — impure clocks drift).
- **Agency**: you can make a continent beat in time.
- **Physics**: Brusselator oscillators, diffusive coupling, drift.
- **Risk**: 8.3 rules learnable (sync intuition is not obvious); G1 rests
  on the toy test — is watching spiral waves lock fun with no goal? Likely
  yes, unproven. 4.5 is a strength: this game IS its music (the one pooled-
  significant lever, g=.60).
- **Profile**: Immersion-Discovery + Creativity-Fantasy; striving play.
- **Gate (intent)**: G1 3 · G2 3 · D1 2 · D3 3 · D4 4.

## 4. The Descent — a roguelike inside a derelict mega-machine

- **Genre**: exploration roguelike (Spelunky / Noita lineage).
- **Fantasy**: a salvage diver inside a continent-sized abandoned machine.
- **Map**: enormous and mostly coarse. The world is GENERATED by the
  hierarchy — regions exist as super-components until you enter them, then
  instantiate in detail. Detail-where-you-look is literally fog of war.
- **Loop**: move (the point agent from the Fields dashboard), read the
  flows, reroute a valve to open a passage or drain a flooded chamber,
  avoid oscillating hazards, salvage castings (blueprints) for the next run.
- **Decision**: risky shortcut through a flooding region vs the long way;
  spend a limited attention budget (only what you inspect is fully
  simulated); which castings to carry.
- **Agency**: you can see the hidden plumbing of a world and turn it.
- **Physics**: flow, chemistry hazards, oscillators; degradation.
- **Risk**: 3.3 fairness — floods must be legible before they kill (8.6
  expectation calibration); randomness must be output-side (world seed at
  run start is fine, mid-run surprises must follow from visible state); G1
  depends on movement feel (4.3 toy test) which is unbuilt.
- **Profile**: Action-Excitement + Immersion-Discovery; achievement play.
- **Gate (intent)**: G1 3 · G2 3 · D1 3 · D3 3 · D4 3.

## 5. Creep Wars — the RTS the project started as

- **Genre**: asymmetric real-time strategy vs AI.
- **Fantasy**: hydraulic engineer vs a spreading hive; the battlefield is
  chemistry.
- **Map**: RTS-scale continent (L2-L3 boards); large maps are the genre
  standard.
- **Loop**: build pumps/dams/reactors; spread or poison creep; flood; hold
  territory as pressure fields shift.
- **Decision**: pressure vs territory; poison (A+B->C kills creep) vs flood;
  commit forces now vs certify infrastructure for cheap later.
- **Agency**: you fight with plumbing.
- **Physics**: flow, chemistry, spread CA, oscillating pumps as artillery.
- **Risk**: 3.1 the AI must be "unrelenting but not overwhelming" — the
  single hardest thing on this list; 8.1 onboarding for a complex genre;
  3.4 workload. Demographic caveat: Competition is #1 at 13-25 and #9 at
  36+ — check S1 against the actual player.
- **Profile**: Action-Excitement + Mastery-Competition; achievement play.
- **Gate (intent)**: G1 3 · G2 4 · D1 3 · D3 2 · D4 3.

## 6. The Inspector — a deduction game about trust

- **Genre**: investigation / deduction (Return of the Obra Dinn / Papers,
  Please lineage, on a continent).
- **Fantasy**: the one honest auditor in a world that runs on trust.
- **Map**: large — many suspects. The whole continent runs on behavioural
  models; components drift, clog (Tier-2), or were falsely certified;
  failures cascade. Only what you inspect is simulated in truth.
- **Loop**: read an anomaly (a region's flow deficit) -> hypothesis -> spend
  inspection budget to device-check a suspect -> confirm/repair -> watch
  the cascade avert (or not).
- **Decision**: which of 300 components to look at with a finite
  inspection budget (compute-as-resource, finally legible); repair now vs
  gather evidence; trust vs verify.
- **Agency (2.6)**: you can see the truth in a world of models. The
  instruments are the project's actual diagnostics: the understanding map,
  device-check, twin tests, spectral audits.
- **Physics**: all of it, plus degradation.
- **Strengths**: 1.3 feedback — every failure teaches a specific lesson
  (this project's own history is the pattern: "the 5% was the drain hop");
  6.3 information gaps are the engine; 6.5 player-authored discovery.
- **Risk**: G1 — is read-then-check fun in 30 seconds with juice off, or
  slow (3.4)? 4.2 diagnostic UIs are juice-poor by default; 8.3 needs
  strong expectation calibration.
- **Profile**: Mastery-Strategy + Immersion-Discovery; striving play.
  Likely the user's own profile.
- **Gate (intent)**: G1 3 · G2 4 · D1 4 · D3 3 · D4 2.

## Recommendation

The rubric's protocol says nothing matters before G1/G2 pass on a greybox
with juice off. Two greyboxes are cheap because their instruments exist:

1. **Foundry Contracts** — the safest G1 (the loop is already playable),
   fastest to test, and the natural on-ramp; but it fits the big-map
   conviction least until late tiers.
2. **The Inspector** — the most distinctive agency on the list, the most
   native to the engine (its tools ARE our diagnostics), and the best fit
   for a huge map that runs coarse everywhere except where you look. Its
   G1 is the open question worth answering first.

These two are also composable with Watershed into one world: build
(Contracts), operate at continent scale (Watershed), audit (Inspector).
Recommend greyboxing Contracts and Inspector on docs/ports.html, five
contracts each, and running the prototype gate with 2-3 raters.
